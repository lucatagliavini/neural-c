## Configuration and compatibility

- `project.conf` accepts optional `gradient_clip_norm`; zero or an absent
  legacy property disables clipping and preserves historical updates and the
  canonical training digest exactly.
- New projects materialize the setting. `init --gradient-clip-norm V` accepts a
  finite non-negative value, and the compile-time-overridable default is zero.
- `inspect` reports the configured value or `disabled`.
- A positive value appends `gradient_clip_norm` and its canonical binary64
  value to training provenance after optional shuffle configuration and before
  optional early-stopping state.
- No weights or checkpoint payload version changes were required.

## Numerical and execution contract

- The reported value is the L2 norm of the finalized mean batch gradient,
  traversed weights then biases in model layer order.
- Stable scaled sum-of-squares accumulation avoids avoidable intermediate
  overflow and underflow; a non-representable result fails before mutation.
- Clipping occurs only when the pre-clipping norm is strictly greater than the
  positive maximum. Equality, zero gradients, and disabled clipping are left
  unchanged.
- The coordinator clips after deterministic reduction and mean calculation.
  The documented future order is reduction, regularization, norm/clipping,
  optimizer transformation, and exclusive model update.
- Worker scheduling and count cannot affect the norm, decision, factor, or
  update. The disabled path performs no gradient copy or scaling.

## Reporting and continuation

- Epoch progress and history report the maximum pre-clipping batch norm and
  clipped batch count. The final result reports the same values for the last
  completed epoch.
- History version 1 adds trailing `gradient_norm` and `clipped_batches` fields;
  it remains diagnostic and outside persistence provenance.
- Resume stores no norm or clipping counter. It regenerates every mean batch
  gradient and repeats the deterministic clipping decision, preserving exact
  continuation across worker counts and architectures.
