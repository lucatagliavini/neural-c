# Session handoff — Milestone 10 scaling benchmark

## Session result

- A bounded native x86-64 training strong-scaling benchmark was completed for
  1, 2, 4, and 8 workers over 1,000, 10,000, and 50,000 samples.
- The full methodology, results, interpretation, and limitations are recorded

- The user authorized committing and pushing the benchmark report and this

- The user-owned untracked `projects/flower3/` directory remains untouched.

## Method and environment

- Revision `4d4a80c`, production `-O2` native binary, GCC 13.3.0.
- Intel Core i7-1365U with 4 physical/8 logical CPUs under WSL2.
- Deterministic dense 32-64-32-8 categorical workload, Adam, full batch, two
  epochs, no shuffle, and no periodic checkpoint.
- One warm-up and five fresh-process wall-time measurements per point; medians
  drive speedup and efficiency calculations.

## Main findings

- Dataset growth is approximately linear after fixed startup costs: from
  10,000 to 50,000 samples, one-worker time grows 4.89x for 5x the work.
- Median 8-worker speedups are 1.218x, 1.415x, and 1.321x for the small, medium,
  and extended datasets respectively.
- Eight-worker average CPU utilization is only 130–142%, consistent with
  synchronization and ordered coordinator work limiting parallelism.
- Peak RSS is about 3.1, 6.0, and 18.3 MiB across the three sizes and changes
  little with worker count for this model.

## Recommended next action

- Use one worker for very small training, four as a balanced default, and eight
  only when a roughly 10–12% latency reduction over four workers is valuable.
- Before implementing performance changes, profile worker synchronization and
  deterministic coordinator accumulation on native Linux or bare metal.
- Keep a broader reproducible benchmark suite, including prediction and wider
  models, for Milestone 12.3 unless field usage makes performance an earlier
  priority.
